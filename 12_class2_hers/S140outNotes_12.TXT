B. REPORT BLOCK FOR ALTERNATIVE MODELING METHODS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Attic
---------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Effect: unconditioned attic above the conditioned zone.
1.2 Options: (a) separate unconditioned zone under a pitched roof; (b) resistance layer.
1.3 Used: (a) - the application's gable roof generator (pitch 18.43 deg, ridge E-W, no eave overhang) creates the attic zone
    (3463 ft3) with its own ventilation rate (2.4 ACH); roof and gable constructions per Table 12-6.

NOTE 2 - Surface heat transfer coefficients
---------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Effect: interior and exterior convection and radiation.
2.2 Options: program-calculated time-varying coefficients, or user constant values.
2.3 Used: program-calculated (TARP inside, DOE-2 outside, detailed long-wave exchange) for all surfaces except: the raised
    floor (combined coefficient fixed at 12.49 W/m2K = 2.2 Btu/h-ft2-F at zero wind, 12.2.1.5) and the soil-side faces of the
    ASHRAE slab/basement resistances (film included in the resistance; see Block C note 4).

NOTE 3 - Basement thermal zoning (L322A, L324A)
---------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Effect: conditioned basement under the main floor.
3.2 Options in 12.2.2.12.1.3: one well-mixed zone, or two zones with identical thermostat control.
3.3 Used: two zones (basement + main floor) with identical heating set point, coupled by the main floor/basement ceiling
    (Table 12-43); results are the sum of both zones' heating loads.

NOTE 4 - Ground coupling of the slab (L302A/L304A)
---------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Used for the reported L302A/L304A results: the ASHRAE perimeter-method slab loss coefficients of Tables 12-36/12-38 as a
    resistance to ambient air. Additional runs L302K/L304K use the application's two-dimensional ground model (Kiva, via the
    project ground setting) with the Annex B18 soil and concrete properties; they are reported for information only.

===============================================================================
C. REPORT BLOCK FOR EQUIVALENT MODELING METHODS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Parallel-path framed constructions
---------------------------------------------------------
Framed and insulated sections are represented by one homogeneous cavity layer whose resistance makes the composite
surface-to-surface R equal the tabulated value (L100A wall 10.901, floor 12.928, ceiling 16.922; L120A wall 22.723,
ceiling 55.962; L200A wall 3.981, floor 3.017, ceiling 10.224 h-ft2-F/Btu); the layer's heat capacity is the area-weighted
capacity of insulation and framing.

NOTE 2 - Interior walls
---------------------------------------------------------
Internal mass with both faces in the zone: 2 x 921.6 ft2 of half the unframed wall (one 0.5 in plasterboard) and
2 x 102.4 ft2 of half the framed wall (1.75 in frame + plasterboard).

NOTE 3 - Windows
---------------------------------------------------------
Each 3 x 5 ft window module = glass 2.542 x 2.156 ft x 2 sashes (10.96 ft2) separated by a 0.23 ft horizontal meeting rail,
plus a 2.75 in frame (frame + rail = 4.04 ft2). Glass: single pane 0.118 in, tau 0.837, rho 0.075, emissivity 0.84,
k 0.52 Btu/h-ft-F (Table 12-11); the program calculates angular properties from these. Frame conductance (surface to surface)
9.096 Btu/h-ft2-F (aluminium, L100A) / 0.756 (wood, L130A); L130A edge-of-glass/center-of-glass conductance ratio 1.153
(Table 12-20). L130A outer low-e pane back-side reflectance taken equal to the front value (0.34) - not given.

NOTE 4 - ASHRAE steady-state ground coupling (L302A, L304A, L322A, L324A)
---------------------------------------------------------
Slab, below-grade wall and basement floor: massless layers equal to the tabulated soil/slab loss resistances (6.564, 15.891,
5.186, 40.614 h-ft2-F/Btu) facing ambient air with a 1000 W/m2K exterior coefficient, no solar and thermal emittance 1e-4.
Each basement wall is divided into rim joist/sill (0.75 ft), above-grade concrete (0.667 ft, both sun exposed) and
below-grade (6.583 ft) sections.

NOTE 5 - Weather
---------------------------------------------------------
Colorad.TMY and Lasvega.TMY converted to EPW by the application's harness converter: radiation re-binned from true solar time to
local standard time (equation of time + longitude correction), diffuse horizontal derived as GHI - DNI cos(z) (absent in the
files), 3-hourly sky cover interpolated, station pressure retained (altitude handled by the program). A run without the solar-
time shift changes loads by less than 0.3 %.

NOTE 6 - Internal gains
---------------------------------------------------------
One equipment object at 5818 Btu/h peak (sensible 4782 + latent 1036) with the Table 12-9 hourly profile; latent fraction
0.178; 70 % of the sensible part radiative. Latent gains have no effect on the reported sensible loads (no latent removal).

Not applicable to Class II (5.2). Values not given by Section 12 that had to be entered: carpet density 12 lb/ft3 and

===============================================================================
G. ANOMALOUS RESULTS
===============================================================================
1. Annual loads are below the minimum example result in all cases but L140AC (heating -0.4 to -12 %, cooling -4 to -17 %).
   The effect of windows is smaller than in the example results (L140A-L100A heating -2.7 MBtu, cooling -25.1 MBtu).
2. L322A/L324A heating is below the example range; in the two-zone model the main floor's gains warm the basement
   (basement mean air temperature 21.9 C).
3. The Kiva slab runs (L302K/L304K) give lower losses than both the ASHRAE perimeter method and the Annex B18 variant.
4. One psychrometric range warning (wet-bulb iteration, 02/12 22:00, Colorado runs) - no effect on results.
