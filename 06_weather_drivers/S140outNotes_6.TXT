   engine EnergyPlus (U.S. DOE), bundled unmodified.

   "energyplus -x -r -w <WDnnn.epw>". Time step: 4 per hour (application
   default; not changed for these tests). Hourly outputs reported.

===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Sky diffuse model
---------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Describe the Effect Being Simulated: Diffuse sky radiation incident on
    tilted and vertical surfaces.

1.2 Optional Settings or Modeling Capabilities:
1.2.1 None user-selectable. The engine always applies its anisotropic sky model
      (isotropic background, circumsolar brightening and horizon brightening,
      Perez-type coefficients).

1.3 Setting or Capability Used: the engine's built-in anisotropic sky model.

NOTE 2 - Weather data format
---------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Describe the Effect Being Simulated: Reading the specified weather data.

1.2 Optional Settings or Modeling Capabilities:
1.2.1 EPW format - the only weather format the engine reads.

1.3 Setting or Capability Used: the equivalent EPW files WD100.epw - WD600.epw
    supplied with the standard (Section 6.2.1.1.1, Informative Note 4; Annex A1
    Table A1-26) were used unchanged. Site latitude, longitude, time zone
    (including the non-integer +5.5 of WD500) and elevation were read from the
    EPW LOCATION record (no Site:Location object in the input); they agree with
    Tables 6-1 and 6-3 to 6-6.

NOTE 3 - Shading calculation
---------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Describe the Effect Being Simulated: Exterior shading / self-shading.

1.3 Setting or Capability Used: Solar Distribution FullInteriorAndExterior (the
    application's Standard 140 profile). The building of Figs 6-1 / 6-2 is
    convex, so no surface shades another (Section 6.2.1.5.2 requires shading to
    be ignored).

NOTE 1 - Alternative building geometry (Section 6.2.1.5)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Describe the Effect Being Simulated: Incident solar radiation on the ten
    surfaces of Table 6-2.

1.2 Section(s) of the Standard where Relevant Inputs are Specified: 6.2.1.4,
    6.2.1.5, Table 6-2, Figures 6-1 and 6-2.

1.3 Equivalent Input(s) Used: The software cannot request incident solar
    without a building model, so the alternative building was modeled. Floor
    plan per Fig 6-1 (8 m x 6 m, south corners cut 2 m x 2 m at 45 degrees),
    walls 2.7 m high (S, 45 deg E of S, E, N, W, 45 deg W of S), roof per
    Fig 6-2: horizontal top at 2.7 + 2 tan 30 = 3.855 m with planes sloped 30
    degrees toward E, S and W, two corner triangles over the 45-degree walls
    and a vertical band on the north side. The 30-degree slope was modeled
    exactly (rise 1.1547 m over 2 m; the figure dimension 1.15 m is rounded).
    The roof planes enclose a separate unconditioned roof-space zone with an
    adiabatic floor at 2.7 m so that both zones are closed. Walls and the
    horizontal/30-degree roof planes report the Table 6-2 orientations.

1.4 Justification: Section 6.2.1.5.2 and its informative note - materials and
    the surfaces not listed in Table 6-2 do not affect the incident solar
    outputs; the shape is convex so there is no self-shading.

NOTE 2 - Ground reflectance
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Describe the Effect Being Simulated: Ground-reflected solar radiation.

1.2 Section(s): 6.2.1.2 (0 for WD100-WD500), 6.2.6.2 (0.2 for WD600).

1.3 Equivalent Input(s) Used: twelve monthly ground reflectances of 0 (WD100 -
    WD500) or 0.2 (WD600). The EPW albedo column is not used by the engine (it
    is 0.2 in WD300.epw and WD500.epw; the reported ground-diffuse incident
    solar of those cases is 0 everywhere, as specified).

1.4 Justification: direct input of the specified value.

G. REPORT BLOCK FOR ANOMALOUS RESULTS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Hourly averages of interpolated values
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Test Case(s) and Specific Results: all cases, all hourly outputs. The
    software interpolates the hourly weather linearly to its 15-minute time
    step and reports hourly means of the four time-step values. Instantaneous
    quantities (dry-bulb, dew point, humidity, pressure, wind, sky cover) are
    placed at the end of their hour; the solar values (averages over the
    preceding hour) are placed at the middle of their hour. The hourly mean of
    an instantaneous quantity therefore equals 0.375 x (previous hour) +
    0.625 x (current hour) - verified to 1e-5 for dry bulb, RH, pressure, wind
    speed and sky cover in every case - and annual solar totals on the
    horizontal are 0.07-0.6 % below the EPW global horizontal totals
    (WD100: 1664.6 vs 1670.2 kWh/m2). Results are entered with time stamps at
    the hour midpoints (averaged values, Section 6.3).

1.2 Explanation: engine time-step interpolation; not an input error.

NOTE 2 - Wind direction interpolated as a scalar
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Test Case(s) and Specific Results: all cases. When the wind direction
    crosses north between two hours (e.g. 310 deg -> 20 deg, WD100 Jan 1
    12:00) the interpolated values pass through the opposite direction (hourly
    mean 173.75 deg). Annual mean wind direction WD100: 173.6 deg reported vs
    179.3 deg in the EPW (933 such hours).

1.2 Explanation: the engine interpolates wind direction linearly without
    taking the short way across 0/360 deg.

NOTE 3 - Psychrometric warnings
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Test Case(s): WD100, WD600 (two warnings each: "Temperature out of range
    (PsyPsatFnTemp)" inside the wet-bulb iteration on 03/13 10:15 and "WetBulb
    not converged" on 03/14 11:45).

1.2 Explanation: engine wet-bulb solver at very dry Denver conditions; the
    run completes; the warnings concern two 15-minute time steps.
