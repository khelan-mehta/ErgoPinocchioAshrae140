B. REPORT BLOCK FOR ALTERNATIVE MODELING METHODS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Surface heat transfer: constant convection coefficients (not the
infiltration alternative of 10.2.1.9)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Effect: heat loss through the roof only, other faces near adiabatic.
1.2 Options: (1) constant user convection coefficients (10.2.1.7);
    (2) infiltration of 0.2 kg/s with all surfaces near adiabatic (10.2.1.9).
1.3 Used: option 1 - SurfaceProperty:ConvectionCoefficients:MultipleSurface,
    roof inside/outside 20 W/m2K, walls and floor inside/outside 0.1 W/m2K.

NOTE 2 - Part-load performance as a tabulated curve
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Effect: furnace cycling losses, PLF = PLR / HIR(PLR) (10.2.1.10.4).
1.2 Options: (1) polynomial curve (Curve:Cubic etc.); (2) Table:Lookup.
1.3 Used: option 2 - Table:Lookup of PLF at PLR = 0, 0.01, ..., 1.00
    (computed from the given HIR coefficients), linear interpolation.
    Applied by the program as fuel = load / (efficiency x PLF) and
    burner run-time fraction = PLR / PLF.

NOTE 3 - Output quantities computed outside the program
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Fuel consumption (m3/s) = total furnace input / 38 MJ/m3 / 7.776E6 s
    (10.3.1.3), computed by the harness from the program's fuel energy.
    Fan energy = circulating fan electricity + furnace on-cycle parasitic
    (draft fan) electricity. Zone temperature mean/max/min are taken over
    the hourly zone mean air temperature, Jan 1 - Mar 31.

C. REPORT BLOCK FOR EQUIVALENT MODELING METHODS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Draft fan
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Effect: 50 W draft fan cycling with the burner, heat to the flue (10.2.2.7).
1.2 Sections 10.2.2.7, 10.2.3.1.2.
1.3 Input: furnace "On Cycle Parasitic Electric Load" = 50 W.
1.4 Justification: the program multiplies this load by the burner run-time
    fraction and adds its electricity to the furnace's electric use; none of
    it enters the airstream or the zone, i.e. exactly a draft fan whose heat
    leaves with the flue gases.

NOTE 2 - Circulating fan power
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Effect: 200 W circulating fan at 0.355 m3/s, 100 % of its heat to the
    supply air (10.2.2.5); 0 W in HE100-HE140.
1.2 Section 10.2.2.5.
1.3 Input: Fan:OnOff total efficiency 1.0, motor efficiency 1.0, motor in
    airstream fraction 1.0, pressure rise = 200 W / 0.355 m3/s = 563.38 Pa
    (0 Pa for 0 W).
1.4 Justification: fan power = flow x pressure rise / efficiency; with
    efficiency 1 and the motor in the airstream the full 200 W is electric
    input and heat to the supply air.

NOTE 3 - Surface emissivity and absorptivity "approaching zero"
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 / 1.2 Section 10.2.1.8.
1.3 Thermal absorptance 0.00001 (smallest legal value), solar and visible
    absorptance 0, on every material.

G. REPORT BLOCK FOR ANOMALOUS RESULTS
===============================================================================
NOTE 1 - Loads 0.5 % above the analytical solution in HE120-HE170
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 HE120 furnace load 31.269 GJ vs analytical 31.10 GJ (all non-saturated
    cases shift by the same factor).
1.2 The program's smallest user convection coefficient is 0.1 W/m2K; walls and
    floor then conduct 1.03 W/K in addition to the specified 199.95 W/K.

NOTE 2 - HE100/HE110 zone temperature 19.75 C
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 HE100, HE110: zone air 19.75 C, furnace at full capacity all period.
1.2 At -30 C the loss at 20 C (201.0 W/K x 50 K = 10.05 kW) slightly exceeds
    the 10 kW capacity, so the furnace runs at full load and the zone settles
    just below set point; load = 10 kW x 7.776E6 s = 77.76 GJ as specified.
